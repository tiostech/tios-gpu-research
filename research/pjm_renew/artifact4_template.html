How it works

Inputs, D−1 morningMeteologica and PJM wind, solar and load forecasts; weather ensembles; Maxar weather at wind and solar sites; sun position; actuals up to D−2
→
Kumo wind, solar and load modelsEach: the last 10,000 hours of its inputs with actual output, ending at D−2
→
Custom inputs for DReplace Meteologica's ALL load, wind and solar; derived stress and duck-curve inputs recomputed; plus custom minus PJM's forecast
→
Kumo price modelProduction's inputs, last 10,000 hours of context with actual prices
→
DA energy price for DHourly median, scored on 2025

No peeking. A forecast for day D only uses what was published by PJM's bid deadline, 11:00 EPT on D−1. Yesterday's actual output is not complete by then, so each Kumo model's context stops at the end of D−2, and its actual-output inputs are at least 48 hours old. The wind, solar and load forecasts fed to the price model were produced day by day from October 2023 onward, so every hour in its context could have existed on that morning.

Why the gap to PJM's forecast. PJM commits generating units on its own load, wind and solar forecasts. When a better forecast disagrees with PJM's, the market can expect PJM to over- or under-commit, and prices may move ahead of time. The price model therefore also gets each custom forecast minus PJM's.
