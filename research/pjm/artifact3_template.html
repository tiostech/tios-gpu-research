Scorecard, 2026 test set

MAE = average absolute hourly error, $/MWh. RMSE weighs big misses more heavily. Gain = production MAE minus Kumo MAE, averaged over days, ± one bootstrap standard error (positive = Kumo better). Days won = share of days where Kumo's daily MAE was lower. Bias = average of actual minus forecast.

Nothing about either model was chosen by looking at 2026. All of the research on Kumo (inputs, context size, ensembles) used 2025 only. The Kumo model here is the original baseline, unchanged: the production model's own inputs plus the date and hour, default settings, the same code and data as the 2025 run. The production forecast is the version actually published each morning.

Same replay as 2025. For each day of 2026, Kumo was given the last 10,000 hours of inputs and actual prices before that day (so 2025 and early 2026 at first, then mostly 2026) and forecast that day's 24 hours. The day's actual prices were added to the context only after it was forecast.

Method note

Data. Target: PJM day-ahead system energy component (pjm_energy_prices.da_energy_price). The test split was cut when the dataset was built, which is why it stops in early October; days after that are not scored here.

Fairness cutoff. Every input for day D is a forecast available by 10:30 EPT on D−1 (PJM day-ahead bids close at 11:00). Day-ahead prices for D−1 clear on D−2, so they are known.

Uncertainty. Paired day-block bootstrap: resample days with replacement (2,000 draws) and recompute the mean daily MAE gain; ± is one standard error.
