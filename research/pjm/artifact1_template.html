Kumo-Tabular beats the production PJM price forecast with the same inputs

Forecast of the hourly PJM day-ahead system energy price for 2025 (the energy component of LMP, without congestion and losses). Both forecasts use only information available by 10:30 EPT the day before, and both use the same input data: the production model's own feature set plus the date and hour.

Kumo here is a no-frills run with default settings. It cut the average hourly error by about 14% and was more accurate on 7 days out of 10, in every season and every block of hours.

Scorecard, 2025

MAE = average absolute hourly error, $/MWh. RMSE weighs big misses more heavily. Gain = production MAE minus Kumo MAE, averaged over days, ± one bootstrap standard error. What Kumo-Tabular is, in plain language

Production forecast

Every morning the production model is trained: random forests and a regression learn a fixed set of rules linking the inputs (load, wind, solar, gas, outages, weather, neighbouring markets) to price over the last two years. The rules are then applied to tomorrow. A third model decides how much to trust each of the two forecasts, hour by hour.

Kumo-Tabular

A large pre-trained foundation model for tables. It is never trained on our data. Each morning we hand it the last ~10,000 hours (about 14 months) of inputs together with the prices that actually cleared, plus tomorrow's 24 rows of inputs. It predicts by recognising which past hours look like tomorrow and how prices behaved then. This is called in-context learning; it works like giving an experienced analyst a stack of comparable days.

What that means in practice

  • New data counts immediately. Yesterday's prices join the context the next morning; nothing has to be refit.
  • No tuning. The run on this page uses default settings and the production model's own inputs.
  • A full range, not one number. It returns quantiles (for example a 10%–90% band) as well as the median scored here. The production forecast is a single number.

Trade-offs

  • It needs a GPU. One day's forecast takes a few seconds on a single data-centre GPU (about 35 minutes for the whole year, replayed day by day).
  • It cannot extrapolate. It reasons from what is in its context, so a price level or condition it has never seen there is hard for it to reach.
  • More inputs are not automatically better. Extra columns that restate the same information dilute the ones that matter; in our research only carefully chosen additions helped.
  • A few extreme days still dominate the remaining error. The ten worst days account for of Kumo's total absolute error, and scarcity spikes are not fully predictable from day-ahead data.

Same result in other markets. With the same set-up (each market's production inputs plus the date and hour), Kumo also beat production in 2025 for MISO (MAE 3.39 vs 3.72, RMSE 6.86 vs 8.45) and SPP (3.93 vs 4.45, RMSE 6.53 vs 7.29).

Method note

Walk-forward. For each day D of 2025, Kumo gets the context (inputs and actual prices) up to the end of D−1 and forecasts D's 24 hours; D's actual prices are then appended and the process moves to D+1. Nothing from D or later is ever in the context.

Fairness cutoff. Every input for day D is a forecast available by 10:30 EPT on D−1 (PJM day-ahead bids close at 11:00). Day-ahead prices for D−1 clear on D−2, so they are known. The production forecast is the version published in pjm_tios_forecasts at 10:30–10:35 EPT on D−1.

Target. PJM day-ahead system energy component (pjm_energy_prices.da_energy_price), the same series the production model targets. 8,759 scored hours (one missing on the DST change).

Held-out days. The 243 days of 2025 whose day number (counting from 1 January = 0) is not a multiple of 3. The other 122 days were used to pick settings in the wider research; the baseline itself was never tuned, so its full-year and held-out numbers are equally clean.

Uncertainty. Paired day-block bootstrap: resample days with replacement (2,000 draws) and recompute the mean daily MAE gain; ± is one standard error.
